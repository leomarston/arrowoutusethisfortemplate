When the owner says "make a copycat", that includes **what they charge for**, not only how it
looks. Measured 2026-09-14 on the photo-cleaner category: the leaders sell the *action*, not the
analysis. Cleanup (716k ratings) scans, groups and sorts for free and meters the **delete** at 5 a
day; Cleaner Kit meters it at 9. Their v6.20 even removed the old watch-an-ad-for-25-more escape.

**Why:** I had built the opposite — delete free and unlimited, charging only for expensive compute
— optimising for the rating. The owner's answer: *"WE ARE NOT A CHARITY."* Ratings are not the
objective function; revenue is. The control group proves the trade is real, not imaginary (Clever
Cleaner gives everything away: 4.78 stars, 5% negatives vs Cleanup's 35%) — but the leader eats
that anger at ten times the scale.

**How to apply:** Read the competitors' free tier out of their own users' reviews before designing
a paywall — the iTunes customer-reviews RSS, both `mosthelpful` and `mostrecent`, deduped by id.
Users state the exact cap and the exact price. Then two rules make the meter shippable rather than
rejectable: disclose it BEFORE the user invests work (a counter on the first screen, again under
the action button), and never refuse everything — spend the remaining allowance and hold back only
the rest. Also check which plan the trial converts to; theirs was the weekly, mine was the annual.
